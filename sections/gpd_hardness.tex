\section{Hardness Results}\label{sec:gpd-hardness}

In this section we provide approximation-hardness results for \textsc{Generalized Conductance}, \textsc{Graph Partitioning with Demands} and \textsc{Hierarchical Clustering with Demands} based on the Small-Set Expansion Hypothesis. The \emph{Small-Set Expansion Hypothesis} states that, for every constant $\zeta>0$, there is a constant $\delta>0$ for which it is NP-hard, given a regular graph, to distinguish between the existence of a vertex set of normalized size $\delta$ and expansion at most $\zeta$, and the condition that every vertex set of normalized size $\delta$ has expansion at least $1-\zeta$. Both reductions in this section use rational numbers instead of natural ones, however this can be fixed by appropriate scaling.

For every unweighted undirected graph $G=(V,E)$ used as a source graph below, write
\[
    \operatorname{vol}_G(S)=\sum_{u\in S}\deg_G(u),
    \qquad
    M_G=\operatorname{vol}_G(V)=2|E|,
\]
\[
    \mu_G(u)=\frac{\deg_G(u)}{M_G},
    \qquad
    \mu_G(S)=\sum_{u\in S}\mu_G(u)=\frac{\operatorname{vol}_G(S)}{M_G},\qquad
    \Phi_G(S)=\frac{|\delta_G(S)|}{\operatorname{vol}_G(S)},
\]
\DDfix{where $\deg_G(u)$ is the \emph{degree} of a vertex $u$ in $G$.}
\DD{do przemyslenia: mamy tutaj konflikty notacyjne: $\mu$, $P$ z wczesniejszymi oznaczeniami.}
For an oriented edge $u\to v$, let $P_G(u,v)=1/\deg_G(u)$ if $uv\in E$ and $P_G(u,v)=0$ otherwise. This is the probability that a random walk at vertex $u$ moves to vertex $v$. Moreover, $\mu\br{u}$ \DD{$\mu_G$?} can be interpreted as the stationary probability that a random walk in $G$ visits vertex $u$. \DD{to dosc nietrywialne (?)}
Now, set
\[
    p_G(u,v)=\mu_G(u)\cdot P_G(u,v).
\]
Thus $p_G(u,v)=p_G(v,u)=1/M_G$ on every edge. Then, for any $S\subseteq V$ we have
\[
    \sum_{uv\in\delta_G(S)}p_G(u,v)
    =\frac{|\delta_G(S)|}{M_G}
    =\mu_G(S)\cdot \Phi_G(S),
    \qquad
    \sum_{uv\in E}p_G(u,v)=\frac12.
\]
When the source graph is clear, we write $\mu(u)$ and $\mu(S)$ for $\mu_G(u)$ and $\mu_G(S)$.

We also recall the Gaussian expansion profile used in the source theorem. For $r\in[0,1)$ and $x\in(0,1)$, let
\[
    \begin{pmatrix}Z\\Z'\end{pmatrix}
    \sim\mathcal{N}\left(
        \begin{pmatrix}0\\0\end{pmatrix},
        \begin{pmatrix}1&r\\r&1\end{pmatrix}
    \right).
\]
Thus $Z$ and $Z'$ are standard Gaussian random variables with correlation $r$. Let $t_x\in\mathbb{R}$ satisfy $\Pr[Z\geq t_x]=x$. We define the expansion profile of the Gaussian noise graph $\mathcal{G}(r)$ by
\[
    \Phi_{\mathcal{G}(r)}(x)
    =\Pr\left[Z'<t_x\mid Z\geq t_x\right]
    =1-\frac{\Pr[Z\geq t_x,Z'\geq t_x]}{x}.
\]

For any graph $H=(V_H,E_H)$ with stationary vertex measure $\mu$, write $\mu(u)=\mu(\{u\})$ and define
\[
    \alpha=\max_{u\in V_H}\mu(u).
\]
We use the standard product-space construction in the source theorem with tensor power $R\geq2$, which gives $\alpha\leq q^{-R}\leq1/4$. This additional atom bound is needed only for the HCD result below, not for the GPD reduction. The explicit parameter choices give a universal constant $K>0$ such that the YES expansion bound $K\varepsilon$ holds uniformly in $q$ for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$.

We first invoke the following result of Raghavendra, Steurer, and Tulsiani~\cite[Theorem~3.5]{ReductionsBetweenExpansionProblems}.
\DD{trzebaby naprawic ``ref'', gdyz rzeczony papier nie ma Thm. 3.5 (jedynie jego arxiv).; trzebaby dodac czym jest $K_\varepsilon$.}

\begin{theorem}[Raghavendra-Steurer-Tulsiani]\label{thm:rst-global-expansion}
    For every integer $q\geq2$ and every $\gamma>0$, assuming the Small-Set Expansion Hypothesis, it is hard to distinguish between the following cases for a graph $H=(V_H,E_H)$ with stationary vertex measure $\mu$, for sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$:
    \begin{description}
        \item[YES:] There is a partition $\cP=\{S_1,\ldots,S_q\}$ of $V_H$ such that, for every $i\in[q]$, 
            $\mu(S_i)=\frac{1}{q}$ and $\Phi_H(S_i)\leq K\varepsilon$.
        \item[NO:] Every $S\subseteq V_H$ satisfies $
            \Phi_H(S)
            >
            \Phi_{\mathcal{G}(1-\varepsilon/2)}(\mu(S))
            -\frac{\gamma}{\mu(S)}$.
    \end{description}
    The output graph can be chosen so that $\alpha\leq q^{-R}\leq1/4$ for its tensor power $R\geq2$.
\end{theorem}

We derive from Theorem~\ref{thm:rst-global-expansion} the form of the gap used in our reduction.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:globalized-long-code-gap}
    Fix an integer $q\geq2$ and a constant $b\in(0,1/2]$. Assuming the Small-Set Expansion Hypothesis, for sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$ it is hard to distinguish between the following cases for a graph $X=(U,F)$:
    \begin{description}
        \item[YES:] There is a partition $\cP=\brc{S_1,\ldots,S_q}$ of $U$
        such that, for every $i\in[q]$, $
            \mu(S_i)=\frac{1}{q}$ and $
            \Phi_X(S_i)\leq K\cdot\varepsilon,$
        where $K>0$ is a universal constant.
        \item[NO:] For every $S\subseteq U$, where $b\leq\mu(S)\leq\frac{1}{2}$, $\Phi_X(S)\geq a_b\cdot \sqrt{\varepsilon},$
        where $a_b>0$ depends only on $b$.
    \end{description}
    The output graph also satisfies $\alpha\leq q^{-R}\leq1/4$ for its tensor power $R\geq2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    In the YES case of Theorem~\ref{thm:rst-global-expansion}, the sets $S_1,\ldots,S_q$ have total measure $1$ and therefore form a partition of $U$. The expansion bound is $K\varepsilon$ by the theorem.

    Consider now the NO case. If $x\in[b,1/2]$, then $t_x$ ranges over a bounded interval depending only on $b$. The standard estimate for the probability that two highly correlated Gaussian variables lie on opposite sides of such a threshold gives $
        \Phi_{\mathcal{G}(1-\varepsilon/2)}(x)
        \geq a'_b\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}$
    for some constant $a'_b>0$ and all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$. Indeed, the standard deviation of $Z'-Z$ is $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$, while the Gaussian density at $t_x$ is bounded above and below by positive constants depending only on $b$. Taking $\gamma\leq b\cdot a'_b\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}/2$ yields
    \[
        \Phi_X(S)\geq\frac{a'_b}{2}\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}
    \]
    whenever $b\leq\mu(S)\leq1/2$. The claim follows with $a_b=a'_b/2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Hardness of Graph Partitioning with Demands}

For simplicity, we carry out the reduction with rational costs and demands. \DDfix{Scaling by a least common denominator does not} affect feasibility or any approximation ratio.

A demand graph is \emph{bipartite multiplicative} if its vertex set has a bipartition $L\mathbin{\dot\cup}R$, its demand edges are all pairs with one endpoint in $L$ and the other in $R$, and there is a potential $\pi$ such that $w(uv)=\pi(u)\cdot \pi(v)$ for every demand edge $uv$. Our intention is to show a reduction which establishes the hardness of graph partitioning with bipartite multiplicative demands.
This class is closed under taking induced subgraphs.


    For every $u\in U$, introduce two vertices $u^{\mathrm L}$ and $u^{\mathrm R}$, assign $
        \pi\br{u^{\mathrm L}}=\pi\br{u^{\mathrm R}}=\mu(u),$ \DD{czym jest $\mu(u)$ tutaj?}
    and let $
        \widetilde U
        =\brc{u^{\mathrm L},u^{\mathrm R}\colon u\in U}.$
    On $\widetilde U$, define the bipartite multiplicative demand graph to have edge set $
        D_\times
        =\brc{u^{\mathrm L}v^{\mathrm R}\colon u,v\in U\times U}$ and demands $
        w\br{u^{\mathrm L}v^{\mathrm R}}
        =\pi\br{u^{\mathrm L}}\cdot \pi\br{v^{\mathrm R}}.$
    For $S\subseteq U$, write $
        \widetilde S=\brc{u^{\mathrm L},u^{\mathrm R}\colon u\in S}.$
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:multiplicative-demands-encode-measure}
    For any $S\subseteq U$, we have, $
        {w\br{\widetilde S}}
        =\mu(S)^2$. In particular, $w\br{\widetilde U}=1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    Every pair $(u,v)\in S\times S$ indexes the unique demand edge $u^{\mathrm L}v^{\mathrm R}$. Therefore,
    \[
        w\br{\widetilde S}
        =\sum_{(u,v)\in S\times S}\pi\br{u^{\mathrm L}}\cdot \pi\br{v^{\mathrm R}}
        =\left(\sum_{u\in S}\mu(u)\right)^2
        =\mu(S)^2.
    \]
    The same calculation for $S=U$ gives $w\br{\widetilde U}=1$.
\end{proof}

We now turn $X$ \DD{czym jest $X$?} into a supply graph $\widetilde X$ on $\widetilde U$. For every $uv\in F$, add the supply edge $u^{\mathrm L}v^{\mathrm L}$ \DDfix{of cost} $c(u^{\mathrm L}v^{\mathrm L})=p_X(u,v)$. For every $u\in U$, also add the \emph{consistency edge} $u^{\mathrm L}u^{\mathrm R}$ \DDfix{of cost} $c(u^{\mathrm L}u^{\mathrm R})=1$. Thus, unless a consistency edge is cut, the two copies of every vertex belong to the same component. Together with the previously defined bipartite multiplicative demand graph on $\widetilde U$, this completes the construction of \DDfix{the graph instance $(\widetilde U,\widetilde X,D_\times,c,w)$.}

\subsubsection{Completenes}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:sse-gpd-completeness}
    Fix $\rho\in(0,1)$ and set $
        q=\max\left\{2,\left\lceil\rho^{-1/2}\right\rceil\right\}. $
    In the YES \DD{yes w ktorym problemie?; czym jest $K$?} case,
    \[
        \OPTdem{\widetilde X}{\rho}\leq \frac{K}{2}\cdot\varepsilon.
    \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    For every $i\in[q]$, define $
        \widetilde S_i
        =\brc{u^{\mathrm L},u^{\mathrm R}\colon u\in S_i}.$
    Since $S_1,\ldots,S_q$ partition $U$, the sets $\widetilde S_1,\ldots,\widetilde S_q$ partition $\widetilde U$. Let $C_{\mathrm{yes}}$ consist of the supply edges $u^{\mathrm L}v^{\mathrm L}$ for which $uv\in F$ has endpoints in distinct parts of the original partition. Thus all consistency edges $u^{\mathrm L}u^{\mathrm R}$ are retained. Every crossing edge of $X$ is counted twice in the sum of the boundaries, and hence
    \[
        c\br{C_{\mathrm{yes}}}
        =\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\mu(S_i)\cdot\Phi_X(S_i).
    \]
    By Lemma~\ref{lem:globalized-long-code-gap},
    \[
        c\br{C_{\mathrm{yes}}}
        \leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\mu(S_i)\cdot K\cdot\varepsilon
        =\frac{K\cdot\varepsilon}{2}.
    \]

    Every component $\widetilde H$ of $\widetilde X\setminus C_{\mathrm{yes}}$ is a union of left-right pairs corresponding to a set $H\subseteq S_i$ for some $i$. Therefore, by Lemma~\ref{lem:multiplicative-demands-encode-measure},
    \[
        \frac{w\br{\widetilde H}}{w\br{\widetilde U}}
        =\mu(H)^2
        \leq\mu(S_i)^2
        =\frac{1}{q^2}
        \leq\rho.
    \]
    Thus $C_{\mathrm{yes}}$ is $\rho$-feasible and has cost at most $\frac{K\cdot\varepsilon}{2}$.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Soundness}
We start by proving a numerical lemma that will be useful in the soundness \DD{co znaczy soundness?} analysis.
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:group-small-components}
    Let $p_1,\ldots,p_m\geq0$ satisfy $
        \sum_{i=1}^{m}p_i=1$ and $
        p_i\leq\sqrt{\eta}<1$ for each $i\in[m]$.
    Define $b_\eta=\min\left\{\frac{1}{4},1-\sqrt{\eta}\right\}$.
    Then, there is a set $I\subseteq[m]$ such that
    \[
        b_\eta\leq\sum_{i\in I}p_i\leq\frac{1}{2}.
    \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    If some $p_i>1/2$, take all indices except $i$. Their sum is at least $1-\sqrt{\eta}\geq b_\eta$ and is less than $1/2$.
    Otherwise, we have $p_i\leq1/2$. If some $p_i\geq b_\eta$, then take $I=\brc{i}$. If all $p_i<b_\eta$, add indices until their sum first reaches $b_\eta$. The resulting sum is less than $2b_\eta\leq1/2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:sse-gpd-soundness}
    In the NO case, every $\eta$-feasible supply-edge set $C$ satisfies $
        c\br{C}\geq\beta_\eta\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}
    $
    for some constant $\beta_\eta>0$ depending only on $\eta$, provided that $\varepsilon$ is sufficiently small.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    Set $\beta_\eta=b_\eta\cdot a_{b_\eta}$. We may assume that $\beta_\eta\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}\leq1$. If $C$ contains a consistency edge, then $c\br{C}\geq1\geq\beta_\eta\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}$, as required.

    Suppose now that no consistency edge is cut. Let $\widetilde H_1,\ldots,\widetilde H_m$ be the components of $\widetilde X\setminus C$. Every $\widetilde H_i$ is a union of left-right pairs. Let $H_i\subseteq U$ be their projection and put $p_i=\mu(H_i)$. The sets $H_1,\ldots,H_m$ partition $U$. By $\eta$-feasibility and Lemma~\ref{lem:multiplicative-demands-encode-measure},
    \[
        p_i^2 = \mu(H_i)^2
        =\frac{w\br{\widetilde H_i}}{w\br{\widetilde U}}
        \leq\eta,
    \]
    and hence $p_i\leq\sqrt{\eta}$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:group-small-components} and let $
        S=\bigcup_{i\in I}H_i.$
    Then $b_\eta\leq\mu(S)\leq1/2$. Every original-copy supply edge corresponding to an edge of $X$ crossing $\br{S,U\setminus S}$ joins two distinct components of $\widetilde X\setminus C$ and therefore belongs to $C$. Consequently, $
        c\br{C}\geq\sum_{(u,v)\in\vec\delta_X(S)}p_X(u,v),
    $ so by Lemma~\ref{lem:globalized-long-code-gap},
    \[
        \sum_{(u,v)\in\vec\delta_X(S)}p_X(u,v)
        =\mu(S)\cdot\Phi_X(S)
        \geq b_\eta\cdot a_{b_\eta}\sqrt{\varepsilon}
        =\beta_\eta\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}.
    \]
    which completes the proof.
\end{proof}
\subsubsection{Hardness of approximation}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:sse-gpd-bicriteria-hardness}
    Fix constants $0<\rho<\eta<1$. Then, for any sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, a universal constant $L$ and a constant $\beta_\eta>0$ dependent only on $\eta$ such that it is SSEH-hard to distinguish between the following two cases:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{YES case:} $\OPTdem{\widetilde X}{\rho}\leq L\cdot\varepsilon$.
        \item \textbf{NO case:} Every $\eta$-feasible supply-edge set $C$ satisfies $c\br{C}\geq\beta_\eta\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}$.
    \end{itemize}
    Consequently, assuming the Small-Set Expansion Hypothesis, for any constant $A>0$ there is no polynomial-time $(A,\eta)$-bicriteria approximation for \textsc{Graph Partitioning with Demands}, even for bipartite multiplicative demands.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Let $L=\frac{K}{2}$. 
    In the YES case, Lemma~\ref{lem:sse-gpd-completeness} gives $\OPTdem{\widetilde X}{\rho}\leq L\cdot\varepsilon$. 
    In the NO case, Lemma~\ref{lem:sse-gpd-soundness} says that every $\eta$-feasible cut has cost at least $\beta_\eta\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}$. 
    Now, suppose that such an algorithm exists. Choose $\varepsilon>0$ sufficiently small that all preceding statements apply and
    \[
        A\cdot L\cdot\varepsilon<\beta_\eta\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}.
    \]
    Equivalently, it is enough to take
    \[
        \varepsilon<\left(\frac{\beta_\eta}{A\cdot L}\right)^2.
    \]
    Thus the algorithm distinguishes the YES and NO instances of Lemma~\ref{lem:globalized-long-code-gap}, contradicting the Small-Set Expansion Hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Hardness of hierarchical clustering with demands}

The preceding reduction concerns flat partitioning. We now use the same expansion-hardness theorem to obtain a gap for the hierarchical objective.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:sse-hcd-hardness}
    Assuming the Small-Set Expansion Hypothesis, for every constant $A>0$ it is hard to approximate \textsc{Hierarchical Clustering with Demands} within a factor of $A$, even when the demand graph is complete and all supply capacities and demand weights are positive integers.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Fix $A>0$ and choose $\varepsilon>0$ sufficiently small that all preceding smallness requirements hold and
    \[
        \varepsilon<\left(\frac{a_{1/4}}{16\cdot A\cdot (K+1)}\right)^2.
    \]
    Let $q$ be the least power of two satisfying $q^{-2}\leq\varepsilon$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:globalized-long-code-gap} with this $q$, $b=1/4$, and $\varepsilon$, and denote its output graph by $H=(V_H,E_H)$ with stationary measure $\mu$.

    Construct an HCD instance whose supply graph is $H$ with capacity $c(uv)=p_H(u,v)$ on each edge $uv\in E_H$. This is well-defined because $p_H(u,v)=p_H(v,u)$. Let the demand graph be complete on distinct vertex pairs, with $w(uv)=\mu(u)\mu(v)$. By defintion, for every $S\subseteq V_H$,
    \begin{equation*}
        c(\delta_H(S))=\mu(S)\cdot \Phi_H(S).
    \end{equation*}
    For every cluster $X\subseteq V_H$,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:hcd-stationary-demand}
        w(X)=\sum_{uv\in D[X]}\mu(u)\cdot \mu(v)
        =\frac12\left(\mu(X)^2-\sum_{u\in X}\mu(u)^2\right).
    \end{equation}
    The shared normalization gives total supply capacity $c(E_H)=1/2$, and we know that $\alpha\leq1/4$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:rst-global-expansion}.

    \emph{YES case.} Make $S_1,\ldots,S_q$ the children of the root of $\cT$, and refine each $S_i$ to singleton leaves. This root may be nonbinary. As noted before, $\cT$ can be made into a binary tree without increasing the cost. The supply edges crossing the root are exactly those between distinct sets, so
    \[
        c(C_{\mathrm{root}})=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^q c(\delta_H(S_i))
        \leq\frac{K\varepsilon}{2}.
    \]
    By~\eqref{eq:hcd-stationary-demand}, $w(V_H)\leq1/2$, and hence the root contributes at most $K\varepsilon/4$ to $c\br{\cT}$. Every supply edge internal to an $S_i$ is charged at a cluster $X\subseteq S_i$, for which $w(X)\leq\mu(X)^2/2\leq1/(2q^2)$. Since the total supply capacity is $1/2$, refining the $S_i$'s contributes at most $1/(4q^2)\leq\varepsilon/4$ to $c\br{\cT}$. Therefore,
    \[
        \operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{YES}}\leq c\br{\cT}
        \leq\frac{K+1}{4}\cdot \varepsilon.
    \]

    \emph{NO case.} Consider any binary clustering tree $\cT$. Starting at its root, follow a larger child of $\cT$ (measured by $\mu(\cdot)$) until first reaching a cluster $S$ with $\mu(S)\leq1/2$. Its parent $P$ has $\mu(P)>1/2$, while the choice of a larger child ensures $1/4<\mu(S)\leq1/2$. The NO case of Lemma~\ref{lem:globalized-long-code-gap}, with $b=1/4$, gives
    \[
        c(\delta_H(S))=\mu(S)\cdot \Phi_H(S)
        \geq\frac{a_{1/4}}{4}\cdot \sqrt{\varepsilon}.
    \]
    By~\eqref{eq:hcd-stationary-demand}, $\sum_{u\in P}\mu(u)^2\leq\alpha\cdot \mu(P)$ and $\alpha\leq 1/4$ we get
    \[
        w(P)\geq\frac{\mu(P)\cdot(\mu(P)-\alpha)}2
        >\frac1{16}.
    \]
    Every supply edge crossing $S$ has its least common ancestor at $P$ or an ancestor of $P$, and is charged at a cluster of demand weight at least $w(P)$. Therefore, for every hierarchy $\mathcal{T}$, the edges crossing $S$ alone contribute
    \[
        c(\mathcal{T})
        \geq\sum_{uv\in\delta_H(S)}c(uv)\cdot
          w\br{V_{\operatorname{lca}_{\mathcal{T}}(u,v)}}
        \geq w(P)\cdot c(\delta_H(S))
        \geq\frac{1}{16}\cdot\frac{a_{1/4}}{4}\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}
        =\frac{a_{1/4}}{64}\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}.
    \]
    Taking the minimum over all trees gives $\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{NO}}\geq\frac{a_{1/4}}{64}\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}$.

    The choice of $\varepsilon$ gives
    \[
        A\cdot\frac{K+1}{4}\cdot\varepsilon
        <\frac{a_{1/4}}{64}\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon},
    \]
    so the YES and NO cases are separated by a factor greater than $A$. Therefore, any $A$-approximation algorithm for \textsc{Hierarchical Clustering with Demands} would distinguish the YES and NO cases, which is SSEH-hard.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Hardness of Generalized Conductance}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:generalized-conductance-hardness}
  For every fixed constant $A>1$, it is NP-hard to approximate \textsc{Generalized Conductance} within a factor of $A$, even when $D=\binom{V}{2}$ and $w(e)=1$ for every $e\in D$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  Let $G=(V,E,c)$ be a supply graph, let $n=|V|$, and let
  \[
\phi_{\mathrm{USC}}\br{S}
=\frac{c\br{S,\bar{S}}}{\spr{S}\cdot\spr{\bar{S}}}
  \]
  be its uniform-demand \textsc{Sparsest Cut} objective. Consider the same supply graph as an instance of \textsc{Generalized Conductance} with $D=\binom{V}{2}$ and $w(e)=1$ for every $e\in D$. For a nontrivial cut $S$, writing $s=\spr{S}$ gives
  \[
w\br{S,V}=\binom{n}{2}-\binom{n-s}{2}
  =\frac{s(2n-s-1)}{2}.
  \]
  Since $1\leq s\leq n-1$, we have
  \[
\frac{n\spr{S}}{2}\leq w\br{S,V}\leq n\spr{S},
  \]
  and the same bounds hold for $\bar{S}$. Therefore,
  \[
\frac{1}{n^2}\phi_{\mathrm{USC}}\br{S}
\leq \psi_w\br{S}
\leq \frac{4}{n^2}\phi_{\mathrm{USC}}\br{S}.
  \]
  Thus, a $\gamma$-approximation for \textsc{Generalized Conductance} on these instances gives a $4\gamma$-approximation for uniform-demand \textsc{Sparsest Cut}. The latter is UGC-hard to approximate within any constant factor~\cite{OnTheHardnessOfApproximatingMulticutAndSparsestCut}; the Unique Games Theorem~\cite{OAI:The-Unique-Games-Theorem-September-23-2026} makes this hardness unconditional, proving the corollary.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Hardness of Graph Partitioning with Demands on Trees}

The following statement gives an approximation-preserving reduction from \textsc{Vertex Cover}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:tree-gpd-vertex-cover-hardness}
For every fixed rational $\rho\in(0,1)$, \textsc{Graph Partitioning with Demands} is as hard to approximate as \textsc{Vertex Cover}, even when the supply graph is a star.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $G=(U,F)$ be a \textsc{Vertex Cover} instance. Let $V=U\cup\{r,d\}$. Construct a supply star $E$ with center $r$, leaves $U$, and one additional leaf $d$. Set $c(rd)=\infty$ and $c(ru)=1$ for every $u\in U$. The edges of the demand graph are $F\cup\{rd\}$ where $w\br{rd}=\frac{\rho \cdot \spr{F}}{1-\rho}$ and, for every $uv\in F$, $w\br{uv}=1$. If necessary, multiply all demand weights by a common denominator to make them integral.

The total demand is $W=w\br{rd}+\spr{F}$, and hence $\rho \cdot W=w\br{rd}$. Any solution of cost at most $\spr{U}$ leaves $rd$ uncut, otherwise its cost becomes $\infty$. For any solution $C$, let $H\in T\setminus C$ be the component containing $r$. If $H$ contained both endpoints of some edge $uv\in F$, its internal demand would exceed $w\br{rd}$. Therefore, the set $C_H=V\setminus V\br{H}$ forms a vertex cover. Moreover, we have that $c\br{C}=\spr{C_H}$ which means that the cost of any solution for the \textsc{Graph Partitioning with Demands} instance equals the size of some vertex cover. Conversely, any cover $C_H\subseteq U$ gives rise to a solution $C$ by cutting all edges incident to $C_H$, which has cost $\spr{C_H}$. Thus the reduction preserves approximation ratios.
\end{proof}

Combining Theorem~\ref{thm:tree-gpd-vertex-cover-hardness} with the known hardness of \textsc{Vertex Cover} gives the following consequence.
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:tree-gpd-hardness}
For every $\varepsilon>0$, \textsc{Graph Partitioning with Demands} on trees is NP-hard to approximate within $2-\varepsilon$~\cite{VertexCoverMightBeHardToApproximateToWithin2MinusEpsilon,OAI:The-Unique-Games-Theorem-September-23-2026}.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Hardness of Hierarchical Clustering with Demands on Trees}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hc-star-hardness}
\textsc{Hierarchical Clustering with Demands} is NP-hard to approximate within a factor of $1.0748$,
even when the supply graph is a star and all capacities and demands are uniform.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We reduce from the NP-hard problem \textsc{Min-Sum Vertex Cover} on general graphs. In this problem we are given an undirected graph $G=(U,F)$ and the goal is to find a permutation $\pi:U\to\{1,\dots,|U|\}$ that minimizes the sum of cover times of the edges $\ct\br{\pi}=\sum_{e\in F}\ct_\pi(e)$, i.e., where the covering time is defined as $\ct_\pi(uv)=\min\{\pi(u),\pi(v)\}$.     Stankovic proves that this problem cannot be approximated within a factor of $1.0748$ assuming the Unique Games Conjecture~\cite{SomeResultsOnApproximabilityOfMinimumSumVertexCover}; the Unique Games Theorem~\cite{OAI:The-Unique-Games-Theorem-September-23-2026} therefore makes this lower bound unconditional. The reduction is very simple. The vertex set is $V=U\cup {r}$, $D=U$ and $E$ is a star with center $r$ and leaves $U$. The capacities and demands are all set to $1$. 

Consider any clustering tree $\cT$. Since any nonbinary tree can be transformed into a binary one without loss of the quality, we assume that $\cT$ is binary. Now, we observe that on stars any binary cut, i.e., cut where both sides of the cut are connected, consists of some leaf $l\in U$ and nothing more. Consequently, any such clustering tree directly corresponds to a sequemce of leaves, in the order they are separated from the root. Conversily, every sequence $\pi:U\to\{1,\dots,|U|\}$ of the leaves corresponds to a unique binary clustering tree. 

Now, for any sequence $\pi:U\to\{1,\dots,|U|\}$ and a vertex $u\in U$, let
\[
E_\pi(u)=\{vw\in F\colon \pi(u)\leq \pi(v), \pi(w)\}
\]
be the set of all edges covered no sooner then $\pi(u)$.
Then, for the clustering tree $\cT$ corresponding to $\pi$ we can rewrite its cost as 
\[
c\br{\cT}=
\sum_{e\in E} \spr{V_{\lca_\cT(e)}}=
\sum_{u\in U} \spr{V_{\lca_\cT(u,r)}}=
\sum_{u\in U}\spr{E_\pi(u)} =
\ct\br{\pi}.
\]
where the third equality is due to the fact that for any vertex $u\in U$, the set $E_\pi(u)$ consists of all edges whose both endpoint appear no sooner then $\pi\br{u}$ in the sequence $\pi$, which are all of the demand edges still present in the cluster containing $u$, before cutting $u$ from the root. The fourth equality follows from basic rewriting of $\ct\br{\pi}$, since each $u\in U$ contributes one per each edge which was not covered by the first $\pi(u)-1$ vertices in the sequence.

Therefore, the costs of corresponding solutions coincide, and the inapproximability of \textsc{Min-Sum Vertex Cover} transfers to \textsc{Hierarchical Clustering with Demands} on supply stars.
\end{proof}
