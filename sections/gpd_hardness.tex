\section{Hardness Results}\label{sec:gpd-hardness}

In this section we provide several approximation-hardness results for \textsc{Generalized Conductance}, \textsc{Graph Partitioning with Demands} and \textsc{Hierarchical Clustering with Demands} based on the Small-Set Expansion Hypothesis and P$\neq$NP. The \emph{Small-Set Expansion Hypothesis} states that, for every constant $\zeta>0$, there is a constant $\delta>0$ for which it is NP-hard, given a regular graph, to distinguish between the existence of a vertex set of normalized size $\delta$ and expansion at most $\zeta$, and the condition that every vertex set of normalized size $\delta$ has expansion at least $1-\zeta$. Both reductions in this section use rational numbers instead of natural ones, however this can be fixed by appropriate scaling.

For every unweighted undirected graph $G=(V,E)$ used as a source graph below, write
\[
    \operatorname{vol}_G(S)=\sum_{u\in S}\deg_G(u),
    \qquad
    M_G=\operatorname{vol}_G(V)=2|E|,
\]
\[
    \mu_G(u)=\frac{\deg_G(u)}{M_G},
    \qquad
    \mu_G(S)=\sum_{u\in S}\mu_G(u)=\frac{\operatorname{vol}_G(S)}{M_G},\qquad
    \Phi_G(S)=\frac{|\delta_G(S)|}{\operatorname{vol}_G(S)},
\]
where $\deg_G(u)$ is the degree of $u$ in $G$. For the simple random walk on $G$, let $P_G(u,v)=1/\deg_G(u)$ if $uv\in E$ and $P_G(u,v)=0$ otherwise. Its stationary distribution is $\mu_G(u)=\deg_G(u)/M_G$: for every $v\in V$,
\[
    \sum_{u\in V}\mu_G(u)P_G(u,v)
    =\sum_{u:uv\in E}\frac{1}{M_G}
    =\frac{\deg_G(v)}{M_G}
    =\mu_G(v).
\]
Thus $\mu_G(u)$ is the probability that the walk is at $u$ at any given step when it is in stationarity.
Now, set
\[
    p_G(u,v)=\mu_G(u)\cdot P_G(u,v).
\]
Thus $p_G(u,v)=p_G(v,u)=1/M_G$ on every edge. Then, for any $S\subseteq V$ we have
\[
    \sum_{uv\in\delta_G(S)}p_G(u,v)
    =\frac{|\delta_G(S)|}{M_G}
    =\mu_G(S)\cdot \Phi_G(S),
    \qquad
    \sum_{uv\in E}p_G(u,v)=\frac12.
\]

We also recall the Gaussian expansion profile used in the source theorem. For $r\in[0,1)$ and $x\in(0,1)$, let
\[
    \begin{pmatrix}Z\\Z'\end{pmatrix}
    \sim\mathcal{N}\left(
        \begin{pmatrix}0\\0\end{pmatrix},
        \begin{pmatrix}1&r\\r&1\end{pmatrix}
    \right).
\]
Thus $Z$ and $Z'$ are standard Gaussian random variables with correlation $r$. Let $t_x\in\mathbb{R}$ satisfy $\Pr[Z\geq t_x]=x$. We define the expansion profile of the Gaussian noise graph $\mathcal{G}(r)$ by
\[
    \Phi_{\mathcal{G}(r)}(x)
    =\Pr\left[Z'<t_x\mid Z\geq t_x\right]
    =1-\frac{\Pr[Z\geq t_x,Z'\geq t_x]}{x}.
\]

For any graph $Y=(V_Y,E_Y)$ define $
    \alpha_Y=\max_{u\in V_Y}\brc{\mu_Y(u)}.
$
%  Note, that this additional bound is needed only for the HCD reduction.
% The explicit parameter choices give a universal constant $K>0$ such that the YES expansion bound $K\cdot\varepsilon$ holds uniformly in $q$ for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$.
We use the following result of Raghavendra, Steurer, and Tulsiani~\cite{ReductionsBetweenExpansionProblems}, noting that the standard product-space construction in the source theorem with tensor power $R\geq2$ certifies that $\alpha_Y\leq q^{-R}\leq1/4$.

\begin{theorem}[Raghavendra-Steurer-Tulsiani]\label{thm:rst-global-expansion}
    For every integer $q\geq2$ and every $\gamma>0$, assuming the Small-Set Expansion Hypothesis, there exists a constant $K>0$ such that for a sufficiently small choice of $\varepsilon>0$ it is hard to distinguish between the following cases for a graph $H=(V_H,E_H)$:
    \begin{description}
        \item[YES:] There is a partition $\cP=\{S_1,\ldots,S_q\}$ of $V_H$ such that, for every $i\in[q]$, 
            $\mu_H(S_i)=\frac{1}{q}$ and $\Phi_H(S_i)\leq K\cdot\varepsilon$.
        \item[NO:] Every $S\subseteq V_H$ satisfies $
            \Phi_H(S)
            >
            \Phi_{\mathcal{G}(1-\varepsilon/2)}(\mu_H(S))
            -\frac{\gamma}{\mu_H(S)}$.
    \end{description}
    The output graph can be chosen so that $\alpha_H\leq q^{-R}\leq1/4$ for its tensor power $R\geq2$.
\end{theorem}

We derive from Theorem~\ref{thm:rst-global-expansion} the form of the gap used in our reduction.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:globalized-long-code-gap}
    Fix an integer $q\geq2$ and a constant $b\in(0,1/2]$. Assuming the Small-Set Expansion Hypothesis, there exist constants $K>0$ and $a_b>0$ such that for a sufficiently small choice of $\varepsilon>0$ it is hard to distinguish between the following cases for a graph $Y=(V_Y,E_Y)$:
    \begin{description}
        \item[YES:] There is a partition $\cP=\brc{S_1,\ldots,S_q}$ of $V_Y$
        such that, for every $i\in[q]$, $
            \mu_Y(S_i)=\frac{1}{q}$ and $
            \Phi_Y(S_i)\leq K\cdot\varepsilon$.
        \item[NO:] For every $S\subseteq V_Y$, where $b\leq\mu_Y(S)\leq\frac{1}{2}$, $\Phi_Y(S)\geq a_b\cdot \sqrt{\varepsilon}$.
    \end{description}
    The output graph also satisfies $\alpha_Y\leq q^{-R}\leq1/4$ for its tensor power $R\geq2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    In the YES case of Theorem~\ref{thm:rst-global-expansion}, the statement stays the same.

    Consider now the NO case. If $x\in[b,1/2]$, then $t_x$ ranges over a bounded interval depending only on $b$. The standard estimate for the probability that two highly correlated Gaussian variables lie on opposite sides of such a threshold gives $
        \Phi_{\mathcal{G}(1-\varepsilon/2)}(x)
        \geq a'_b\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}$
    for some constant $a'_b>0$ and all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$. Indeed, the standard deviation of $Z'-Z$ is $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$, while the Gaussian density at $t_x$ is bounded above and below by positive constants depending only on $b$. Taking $\gamma\leq b\cdot a'_b\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}/2$ yields
    \[
        \Phi_Y(S)\geq a'_b\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon} - \frac{b\cdot a'_b\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}/2}{1}
        = a'_b\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon} - \frac{b\cdot a'_b\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}}{2}
        = a'_b\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}\left(1 - \frac{b}{2}\right)
        \geq \frac{a'_b}{2}\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}
    \]
    whenever $b\leq\mu_Y(S)\leq1/2$. The claim follows with $a_b=a'_b/2$.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Completenes}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:sse-gpd-completeness}
    Fix $\rho\in(0,1)$ and set $
        q=\max\brc{2,\left\lceil\rho^{-1/2}\right\rceil}. $
    In the YES case of Lemma~\ref{lem:globalized-long-code-gap},
    \[
        \OPTdem{\widetilde Y}{\rho}\leq \frac{K}{2}\cdot\varepsilon.
    \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    For every $i\in[q]$, define $
        \widetilde S_i
        =\brc{u^{\mathrm L},u^{\mathrm R}\colon u\in S_i}.$
    Since $S_1,\ldots,S_q$ partition $V_Y$, the sets $\widetilde S_1,\ldots,\widetilde S_q$ partition $\widetilde V_Y$. Let $C_{\mathrm{yes}}$ consist of the supply edges $u^{\mathrm L}v^{\mathrm L}$ for which $uv\in E_Y$ has endpoints in distinct parts of the original partition. Thus all consistency edges $u^{\mathrm L}u^{\mathrm R}$ are retained. Every crossing edge of $Y$ is counted twice in the sum of the boundaries, and hence
    \[
        c\br{C_{\mathrm{yes}}}
        =\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\mu_Y(S_i)\cdot\Phi_Y(S_i).
    \]
    By Lemma~\ref{lem:globalized-long-code-gap},
    \[
        c\br{C_{\mathrm{yes}}}
        \leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\mu_Y(S_i)\cdot K\cdot\varepsilon
        =\frac{K\cdot\varepsilon}{2}.
    \]

    Every component $\widetilde H$ of $\widetilde Y\setminus C_{\mathrm{yes}}$ is a union of left-right pairs corresponding to a set $H\subseteq S_i$ for some $i$. Therefore, by Lemma~\ref{lem:multiplicative-demands-encode-measure},
    \[
        \frac{w\br{\widetilde H}}{w\br{\widetilde V_Y}}
        =\mu_Y(H)^2
        \leq\mu_Y(S_i)^2
        =\frac{1}{q^2}
        \leq\rho.
    \]
    Thus $C_{\mathrm{yes}}$ is $\rho$-feasible and has cost at most $\frac{K\cdot\varepsilon}{2}$.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Soundness}
We start by proving a numerical lemma that will be useful in the soundness analysis.
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:group-small-components}
    Let $p_1,\ldots,p_m\geq0$ satisfy $
        \sum_{i=1}^{m}p_i=1$ and $
        p_i\leq\sqrt{\eta}<1$ for each $i\in[m]$.
    Define $b_\eta=\min\brc{\frac{1}{4},1-\sqrt{\eta}}$.
    Then, there is a set $I\subseteq[m]$ such that
    \[
        b_\eta\leq\sum_{i\in I}p_i\leq\frac{1}{2}.
    \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    If some $p_i>1/2$, take all indices except $i$. Their sum is at least $1-\sqrt{\eta}\geq b_\eta$ and is less than $1/2$.
    Otherwise, we have $p_i\leq1/2$. If some $p_i\geq b_\eta$, then take $I=\brc{i}$. If all $p_i<b_\eta$, add indices until their sum first reaches $b_\eta$. The resulting sum is less than $2b_\eta\leq1/2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:sse-gpd-soundness}
    In the NO case, every $\eta$-feasible supply-edge set $C$ satisfies $
        c\br{C}\geq\beta_\eta\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}
    $
    for some constant $\beta_\eta>0$ depending only on $\eta$, provided that $\varepsilon$ is sufficiently small.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    Set $\beta_\eta=b_\eta\cdot a_{b_\eta}$. We may assume that $\beta_\eta\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}\leq1$. If $C$ contains a consistency edge, then $c\br{C}\geq1\geq\beta_\eta\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}$, as required.

    Suppose now that no consistency edge is cut. Let $\widetilde H_1,\ldots,\widetilde H_m$ be the components of $\widetilde Y\setminus C$. Every $\widetilde H_i$ is a union of left-right pairs. Let $H_i\subseteq V_Y$ be their projection and put $p_i=\mu_Y(H_i)$. The sets $H_1,\ldots,H_m$ partition $V_Y$. By $\eta$-feasibility and Lemma~\ref{lem:multiplicative-demands-encode-measure},
    \[
        p_i^2 = \mu_Y(H_i)^2
        =\frac{w\br{\widetilde H_i}}{w\br{\widetilde V_Y}}
        \leq\eta,
    \]
    and hence $p_i\leq\sqrt{\eta}$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:group-small-components} and let $
        S=\bigcup_{i\in I}H_i.$
    Then $b_\eta\leq\mu_Y(S)\leq1/2$. Every original-copy supply edge corresponding to an edge of $Y$ crossing $\br{S,V_Y\setminus S}$ joins two distinct components of $\widetilde Y\setminus C$ and therefore belongs to $C$. Consequently, $
        c\br{C}\geq\sum_{(u,v)\in\vec\delta_Y(S)}p_Y(u,v),
    $ so by Lemma~\ref{lem:globalized-long-code-gap},
    \[
        \sum_{(u,v)\in\vec\delta_Y(S)}p_Y(u,v)
        =\mu_Y(S)\cdot\Phi_Y(S)
        \geq b_\eta\cdot a_{b_\eta}\sqrt{\varepsilon}
        =\beta_\eta\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}.
    \]
    which completes the proof.
\end{proof}
\subsubsection{Hardness of approximation}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:sse-gpd-bicriteria-hardness}
    Fix constants $0<\rho<\eta<1$. Then, for any sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, a universal constant $L$ and a constant $\beta_\eta>0$ dependent only on $\eta$ such that it is SSEH-hard to distinguish between the following two cases:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{YES case:} $\OPTdem{\widetilde Y}{\rho}\leq L\cdot\varepsilon$.
        \item \textbf{NO case:} Every $\eta$-feasible supply-edge set $C$ satisfies $c\br{C}\geq\beta_\eta\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}$.
    \end{itemize}
    Consequently, assuming the Small-Set Expansion Hypothesis, for any constant $A>0$ there is no polynomial-time $(A,\eta)$-bicriteria approximation for \textsc{Graph Partitioning with Demands}, even for bipartite multiplicative demands.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Let $L=\frac{K}{2}$. 
    In the YES case, Lemma~\ref{lem:sse-gpd-completeness} gives $\OPTdem{\widetilde Y}{\rho}\leq L\cdot\varepsilon$.
    In the NO case, Lemma~\ref{lem:sse-gpd-soundness} says that every $\eta$-feasible cut has cost at least $\beta_\eta\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}$. 
    Now, suppose that such an algorithm exists. Choose $\varepsilon>0$ sufficiently small that all preceding statements apply and
    \[
        A\cdot L\cdot\varepsilon<\beta_\eta\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}.
    \]
    Equivalently, it is enough to take
    \[
        \varepsilon<\left(\frac{\beta_\eta}{A\cdot L}\right)^2.
    \]
    Thus the algorithm distinguishes the YES and NO instances of Lemma~\ref{lem:globalized-long-code-gap}, contradicting the Small-Set Expansion Hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Hardness of Hierarchical Clustering with Demands}

% We use the same expansion-hardness theorem to obtain a gap for the hierarchical objective.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:sse-hcd-hardness}
    Assuming the Small-Set Expansion Hypothesis, for every constant $A\geq 1$ it is hard to approximate \textsc{Hierarchical Clustering with Demands} within a factor of $A$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Fix $A\geq 1$ and choose $\varepsilon>0$ sufficiently small that all preceding smallness requirements hold and
    \[
        \varepsilon<\left(\frac{a_{1/4}}{16\cdot A\cdot (K+1)}\right)^2.
    \]
    Let $q$ be the least power of two satisfying $q^{-2}\leq\varepsilon$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:globalized-long-code-gap} with this $q$, $b=1/4$, and $\varepsilon$, and denote its output graph by $Y=(V_Y,E_Y)$.

    Construct an HCD instance whose supply graph is $Y$ with capacity $c(uv)=p_Y(u,v)$ on each edge $uv\in E_Y$. This is well-defined because $p_Y(u,v)=p_Y(v,u)$. Let the demand graph be complete on distinct vertex pairs, with $w(uv)=\mu_Y(u)\cdot \mu_Y(v)$. By defintion, for every $S\subseteq V_Y$, we have $
        c(\delta_Y(S))=\mu_Y(S)\cdot \Phi_Y(S).$
    For every cluster $X\subseteq V_Y$,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:hcd-stationary-demand}
        w(X)=\sum_{uv\in D[X]}\mu_Y(u)\cdot \mu_Y(v)
        =\frac12\left(\mu_Y(X)^2-\sum_{u\in X}\mu_Y(u)^2\right).
    \end{equation}
    The shared normalization gives total supply capacity $c(E_Y)=1/2$, and we can assume that $\alpha_Y\leq1/4$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:rst-global-expansion}.

    \emph{YES case.} Make $S_1,\ldots,S_q$ the children of the root of $\cT$, and refine each $S_i$ to singleton leaves. This tree may be nonbinary, however as noted before, $\cT$ can be made into a binary tree without increasing the cost. Summing over the supply edges crossing the root partition we have that
    \[
        c(C_{\mathrm{root}})=\frac12\cdot\sum_{i=1}^q c(\delta_Y(S_i))
        \leq\frac{K\cdot\varepsilon}{2}.
    \]
    By~\eqref{eq:hcd-stationary-demand}, $w(V_Y)\leq\mu_Y\br{V_Y}^2/2=1/2$, and hence the root partition contributes at most $K\cdot\varepsilon/4$ to $c\br{\cT}$. Every supply edge internal to an $S_i$ is charged at a cluster $X\subseteq S_i$, for which $w(X)\leq\mu_Y(X)^2/2\leq1/(2q^2)$. Since the total supply capacity is $1/2$, refining the $S_i$'s contributes at most $1/(4q^2)\leq\varepsilon/4$ to $c\br{\cT}$. Therefore,
    \[
        \operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{YES}}\leq c\br{\cT}
        \leq\frac{K+1}{4}\cdot \varepsilon.
    \]

    \emph{NO case.} Consider any binary clustering tree $\cT$. Starting at its root, follow a larger child of $\cT$ (measured by $\mu_Y(\cdot)$) until first reaching a cluster $S$ with $\mu_Y(S)\leq1/2$. Its parent $P$ has $\mu_Y(P)>1/2$, while the choice of a larger child ensures $1/4<\mu_Y(S)\leq1/2$. The NO case of Lemma~\ref{lem:globalized-long-code-gap}, with $b=1/4$, gives
    \[
        c(\delta_Y(S))=\mu_Y(S)\cdot \Phi_Y(S)
        \geq\frac{a_{1/4}}{4}\cdot \sqrt{\varepsilon}.
    \]
    Using~\eqref{eq:hcd-stationary-demand}, $\sum_{u\in P}\mu_Y(u)^2\leq\alpha_Y\cdot \mu_Y(P)$ and $\alpha_Y\leq 1/4$ we get
    \[
        w(P)\geq\frac{\mu_Y(P)\cdot(\mu_Y(P)-\alpha_Y)}2
        >\frac1{16}.
    \]
    Every supply edge crossing $S$ has its least common ancestor at $P$ or an ancestor of $P$, and is charged at a cluster of demand weight at least $w(P)$. Therefore, for every hierarchy $\mathcal{T}$, the edges crossing $S$ alone contribute
    \[
        c(\mathcal{T})
        \geq\sum_{uv\in\delta_Y(S)}c(uv)\cdot
          w\br{V_{\operatorname{lca}_{\mathcal{T}}(u,v)}}
        \geq c(\delta_Y(S))\cdot w(P)
        \geq\frac{1}{16}\cdot\frac{a_{1/4}}{4}\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}
        =\frac{a_{1/4}}{64}\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}.
    \]
    Taking the minimum over all trees gives $\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{NO}}\geq\frac{a_{1/4}}{64}\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}$.

    By our choice of $\varepsilon$, we have
    \[
        A\cdot\frac{K+1}{4}\cdot\varepsilon
        <\frac{a_{1/4}}{64}\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon},
    \]
    so the YES and NO cases are separated by a factor greater than $A$. Therefore, any $A$-approximation algorithm for \textsc{Hierarchical Clustering with Demands} would distinguish the YES and NO cases, which is SSEH-hard.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:sse-gpd-bicriteria-hardness}
    Assuming the Small-Set Expansion Hypothesis, for every fixed $0<\rho<\eta<1$ and every constant $A\geq 1$, there is no polynomial-time $(A,\eta)$-bicriteria approximation for \textsc{Graph Partitioning with Demands} with target $\rho$, even when the demand graph is complete.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
    Suppose such an algorithm exists. Applying Theorem~\ref{thm:hc-gpd-transfer} with these $\rho$ and $\eta$ would give a polynomial-time
    $\frac{\lceil1/\rho\rceil A}{1-\eta}$-approximation for \textsc{Hierarchical Clustering with Demands}. This factor is constant for fixed $\rho$, $\eta$, and $A$, contradicting Theorem~\ref{thm:sse-hcd-hardness}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Hardness of Generalized Conductance}
The hardness of generalized conductance essentially follows from the previous hardness results for GPD, however the recent result of OpenAI \cite{OAI:Constant-factor-hardness-of-uniform-sparsest-cut-September-24-2026} gives a constant-factor approximation hardness for uniform-demand \textsc{Sparsest Cut}, which translates to a constant-factor approximation hardness for \textsc{Generalized Conductance} with uniform demands.
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:generalized-conductance-hardness}
  For every fixed constant $A>1$, it is NP-hard to approximate \textsc{Generalized Conductance} within a factor of $A$, even when $D=\binom{V}{2}$ and $w(e)=1$ for every $e\in D$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  Let $G=(V,E,c)$ be a supply graph, let $n=|V|$, and recall that the uniform-demand \textsc{Sparsest Cut} objective is
  \[
\phi\br{S}
=\frac{c\br{S,\bar{S}}}{\spr{S}\cdot\spr{\bar{S}}}
  \]
  Consider the same supply graph as an instance of \textsc{Generalized Conductance} with $D=\binom{V}{2}$ and $w(e)=1$ for every $e\in D$. For a nontrivial cut $S$, writing $s=\spr{S}$ gives
  \[
w\br{S,V}=\binom{n}{2}-\binom{n-s}{2}
  =\frac{s\cdot(2n-s-1)}{2}.
  \]
  Since $1\leq s\leq n-1$, we have
  \[
\frac{n\cdot \spr{S}}{2}\leq w\br{S,V}\leq n\cdot \spr{S},
  \]
  and the same bounds hold for $\bar{S}$. Therefore,
  \[
\frac{\phi\br{S}}{n^2}
\leq \psi_w\br{S}
\leq \frac{4\cdot \phi\br{S}}{n^2}.
  \]
  Thus, a $\gamma$-approximation for \textsc{Generalized Conductance} on these instances gives a $4\gamma$-approximation for uniform-demand \textsc{Sparsest Cut}. Since uniform-demand \textsc{Sparsest Cut} is NP-hard to approximate within any constant factor~\cite{OAI:Constant-factor-hardness-of-uniform-sparsest-cut-September-24-2026}, the proof is complete.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Hardness of Graph Partitioning with Demands on Stars}

The following statement gives an approximation-preserving reduction from \textsc{Vertex Cover} for the case when the supply graph is a star.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:tree-gpd-vertex-cover-hardness}
For every fixed rational $\rho\in(0,1)$ and $\varepsilon>0$ the \textsc{Graph Partitioning with Demands} is NP-hard to approximate within $2-\varepsilon$ factor, even when the supply graph is a star.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $G=(U,F)$ be a \textsc{Vertex Cover} instance. Let $V=U\cup\{r,d\}$. Construct a supply star $E$ with center $r$, leaves $U$, and one additional leaf $d$. Set $c(rd)=\infty$ and $c(ru)=1$ for every $u\in U$. The edges of the demand graph are $F\cup\{rd\}$ where $w\br{rd}=\frac{\rho \cdot \spr{F}}{1-\rho}$ and, for every $uv\in F$, $w\br{uv}=1$. If necessary, multiply all demand weights by a common denominator to make them integral.

The total demand is $W=w\br{rd}+\spr{F}$, and hence $\rho \cdot W=w\br{rd}$. Any solution of cost at most $\spr{U}$ leaves $rd$ uncut, otherwise its cost becomes $\infty$. For any solution $C$, let $H\in T\setminus C$ be the component containing $r$. If $H$ contained both endpoints of some edge $uv\in F$, its internal demand would exceed $w\br{rd}$. Therefore, the set $C_H=V\setminus V\br{H}$ forms a vertex cover. Moreover, we have that $c\br{C}=\spr{C_H}$ which means that the cost of any solution for the \textsc{Graph Partitioning with Demands} instance equals the size of some vertex cover. Conversely, any cover $C_H\subseteq U$ gives rise to a solution $C$ by cutting all edges incident to $C_H$, which has cost $\spr{C_H}$. Thus the reduction preserves approximation ratios.

Combining this, with the recently proved Unique Games Theorem \cite{OAI:The-Unique-Games-Theorem-September-23-2026} which implies that \textsc{Vertex Cover} is hard to approximate within $2-\varepsilon$ factor yields the proof.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Hardness of Hierarchical Clustering with Demands on Stars}
A similar result as above, however based on the hardness of \textsc{Min-Sum Vertex Cover} gives the following result.
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hc-star-hardness}
\textsc{Hierarchical Clustering with Demands} is NP-hard to approximate within a factor of $1.0748$,
even when the supply graph is a star and all capacities and demands are uniform.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We reduce from the NP-hard problem \textsc{Min-Sum Vertex Cover} on general graphs. In this problem we are given an undirected graph $G=(U,F)$ and the goal is to find a permutation $\pi:U\to\{1,\dots,|U|\}$ that minimizes the sum of cover times of the edges $\ct\br{\pi}=\sum_{e\in F}\ct_\pi(e)$, i.e., where the covering time is defined as $\ct_\pi(uv)=\min\brc{\pi(u),\pi(v)}$. Stankovic proves that this problem cannot be approximated within a factor of $1.0748$ assuming the Unique Games Conjecture~\cite{SomeResultsOnApproximabilityOfMinimumSumVertexCover}, the Unique Games Theorem~\cite{OAI:The-Unique-Games-Theorem-September-23-2026} therefore makes this lower bound conditional on P versus NP. The reduction is very simple. The vertex set is $V=U\cup {r}$, $D=U$ and $E$ is a star with center $r$ and leaves $U$. The capacities and demands are all set to $1$.

Consider any clustering tree $\cT$. Since any nonbinary tree can be transformed into a binary one without loss of the quality, we assume that $\cT$ is binary. Now, we observe that on stars any binary cut, i.e., cut where both sides of the cut are connected, consists of some leaf $l\in U$ and nothing more. Consequently, any such clustering tree directly corresponds to a sequemce of leaves, in the order they are separated from the root. Conversily, every sequence $\pi:U\to\{1,\dots,|U|\}$ of the leaves corresponds to a unique binary clustering tree. 

Now, for any sequence $\pi:U\to\{1,\dots,|U|\}$ and a vertex $u\in U$, let
\[
E_\pi(u)=\{vw\in F\colon \pi(u)\leq \pi(v), \pi(w)\}
\]
be the set of all edges covered no sooner then $\pi(u)$.
Then, for the clustering tree $\cT$ corresponding to $\pi$ we can rewrite its cost as 
\[
c\br{\cT}=
\sum_{e\in E} \spr{V_{\lca_\cT(e)}}=
\sum_{u\in U} \spr{V_{\lca_\cT(u,r)}}=
\sum_{u\in U}\spr{E_\pi(u)} =
\ct\br{\pi}.
\]
where the third equality is due to the fact that for any vertex $u\in U$, the set $E_\pi(u)$ consists of all edges whose both endpoint appear no sooner then $\pi\br{u}$ in the sequence $\pi$, which are all of the demand edges still present in the cluster containing $u$, before cutting $u$ from the root. The fourth equality follows from basic rewriting of $\ct\br{\pi}$, since each $u\in U$ contributes one per each edge which was not covered by the first $\pi(u)-1$ vertices in the sequence.

Therefore, the costs of corresponding solutions coincide, and the inapproximability of \textsc{Min-Sum Vertex Cover} transfers to \textsc{Hierarchical Clustering with Demands} on supply stars.
\end{proof}
