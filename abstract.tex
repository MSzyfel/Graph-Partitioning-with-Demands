\begin{abstract}
  We study various graph partitioning problems under a general demand model. In each such task, we are given a graph $G=(V,E,D, c,w)$, where $E$ are the supply and $D$ are the demand edges, with a capacity function $c\colon E\to \mathbb{N}$ and a demand function $w\colon D\to \mathbb{N}$, and the goal is to separate the graph into parts while achieving some kind of balance, according to the cost measure.
  Our main focus is the problem of finding a cut $(S, \bar{S})$ minimizing the \emph{generalized conductance}
  \[
  \psi_w( S ) = \frac{c( \delta_E\br{S})}{w( S, V )\cdot w( \bar{S}, V )}.
  \]
  where $w( S, V )=w( S )+w( S,\bar{S})$. We call the problem of minimizing $\psi_w( S )$ the \textsc{Generalized Conductance} problem. We give an $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\log n})$-approximation algorithm for general graphs and an exact polynomial-time algorithm for supply trees. The general-graph result follows by comparing the objective with degree-weighted sparsity and reducing the latter to \textsc{Min-Ratio Vertex Cut}. For supply trees, we show that an optimum is attained by a cut that separates one edge.
  
  Moreover, we leverage this result to obtain an $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\log n})$-bicriteria approximation for \textsc{Graph Partitioning with Demands}, where the goal is to find a minimum-cost subset of edges $C$ such that for every component $H$ of $G\setminus C$, $w( H )\leq \rho\cdot w( V )$. This, in turn, yields an $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\log n})$-approximation for \textsc{Hierarchical Clustering with Demands}, the problem of finding a hierarchy of cuts that partitions the graph into increasingly refined clusters. For supply trees, these guarantees can be improved to small constant factors for both objectives.

  To complement these, we prove that \textsc{Generalized Conductance} and \textsc{Graph Partitioning with Demands} are both SSEH-hard to approximate within a factor of $\Omega(\sqrt{\log\log n})$. Moreover, even for support star instances, beating a factor of $2$ for the latter task is UGC-hard.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Graph partitioning with demands, Generalized conductance, Sparsest cut, Hierarchical clustering, Approximation algorithms.
