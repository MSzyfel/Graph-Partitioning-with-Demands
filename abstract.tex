\begin{abstract}
  We study various graph partitioning problems under a general demand model. In each such task, we are given a graph with two edge sets $G=(V,E,D, c,w)$, where $E$ are the supply and $D$ are the demand edges, with a capacity function $c\colon E\to \mathbb{N}$ and a demand function $w\colon D\to \mathbb{N}$, and the goal is to separate the graph into parts while achieving some kind of balance, according to the cost measure.
  Our main focus is the problem of finding a cut $(S, \bar{S})$ minimizing the \emph{generalized conductance}
  \[
  \psi_w( S ) = \frac{c( \delta_E\br{S})}{w( S, V )\cdot w( \bar{S}, V )}.
  \]
  where $w( S, V )=w( S )+w(\delta_D\br{S})$. We call the problem of minimizing $\psi_w( S )$ the \textsc{Generalized Conductance} problem. We give an $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\log n})$-approximation algorithm for general graphs by reducing the problem to the \textsc{Min-Ratio Vertex Cut} and we show that for supply trees, an optimum is attained by a cut that separates one edge, yielding a polynomial-time algorithm.
  
  Moreover, we leverage this result to obtain an $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\log n})$-bicriteria approximation for \textsc{Graph Partitioning with Demands}, where the goal is to find a minimum-cost subset of edges $C$ such that for every component $H$ of $G\setminus C$, $w( H )\leq \rho\cdot w( V )$. This, in turn, yields an $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\log n})$-approximation for \textsc{Hierarchical Clustering with Demands}, the problem of finding a hierarchy of cuts that partitions the graph into increasingly refined clusters. For supply trees, these guarantees can be improved to small constant factors, using LP-based techniques or a greedy recurson combined with the $1-1/e$-approximation for \textsc{Budgeted Maximum Coverage}.

  To complement these results, we prove that \textsc{Generalized Conductance} and \textsc{Graph Partitioning with Demands} are both hard to approximate within any constant factor under the Small-Set Expansion Hypothesis, even for bipartite multiplicative demands. Moreover, even for support star instances, beating a factor of $2$ for the latter task is UGC-hard.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Graph partitioning with demands, Generalized conductance, Sparsest cut, Hierarchical clustering, Approximation algorithms.
