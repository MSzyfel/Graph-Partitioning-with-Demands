\begin{abstract}
We study various graph partitioning tasks subject to general partitioning demands. In \textsc{Graph Partitioning with Demands} (GPD), the goal is to delete supply edges of minimum total cost so that each remaining component contains at most a prescribed fraction of the total demand. In \textsc{Hierarchical Clustering with Demands} (HCD), the goal is to build a hierarchy of clusters, charging each supply edge by the demand in the smallest cluster containing its endpoints.

Our main general-graph results are approximation algorithms for GPD and HCD. For every fixed $0<\rho<\eta<1$, we give a polynomial-time bi-criteria algorithm for GPD whose components have demand at most $\eta \cdot W$ and whose cost is at most $\mathcal{O}_{\rho,\eta}(\sqrt{\log n})$ times the optimum cost for threshold $\rho$. This algorithm can be then recursively used to obtain an $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\log n})$-approximation for HCD. The algorithm for GPD itself iteratively uses a crucial subproblem called \textsc{Generalized Conductance} (GC), which is a new generalization of the unit-demand sparsest cut. The latter is then reducible to the well-known \textsc{Min-Ratio Vertex Cut} problem.

On supply trees, we show that GC can be solved exactly in polynomial time, which leads to improved algorithms for GPD and HCD on trees. We also give bi-criteria algorithms for GPD based on anchored-LP rounding and separator recursion, as well as a single-criterion $(4+\epsilon)$-approximation in $n^{O(1/\rho)}$ time. The GPD algorithms yield a $\frac{4e}{e-1}$-approximation for HCD on trees. \textsc{Generalized Conductance} is NP-hard to approximate within any constant factor, even with unit demand on every pair of vertices~\cite{OAI:Constant-factor-hardness-of-uniform-sparsest-cut-September-24-2026}. Assuming SSEH, we prove that HCD is hard to approximate within any constant factor even with complete demands; this implies that GPD has no constant-factor bi-criteria approximation for any fixed $0<\rho<\eta<1$. On trees, GPD is NP-hard to approximate within $2-\epsilon$, while HCD is NP-hard to approximate within $1.0748$. The tree hardness bounds follow from the Unique Games Theorem~\cite{OAI:The-Unique-Games-Theorem-September-23-2026}.
\end{abstract}
